Civilian casualty mitigation in targeting operations

hyperdoc

Civilian-harm mitigation is an operational capacity, not a slogan. It depends on staffing, review time, current intelligence, escalation paths, and the authority to delay or abort a strike when civilian risk is unresolved. HyperDoc uses this page to keep mitigation capacity distinct from after-the- fact regret.

What mitigation capacity does

  • checks current civilian presence and site status
  • forces explicit review of collateral-risk assumptions
  • creates channels for dissent, delay, or abort
  • documents the reasoning behind approval under uncertainty

How reduced staffing changes outcomes

When staffing and review capacity are reduced, mitigation becomes thinner in practice even if formal policy language stays the same. Fewer reviewers, shorter timelines, or weaker institutional emphasis can turn mitigation into a box-check rather than a real constraint on action.

What mitigation is not

Mitigation is not the same as public-relations damage control after a strike. It is the ex ante capacity to reduce the chance that civilians are misread, overlooked, or treated as acceptable collateral before launch.

Boundary

This page does not claim that more staffing alone solves targeting mistakes. It preserves a narrower point: weakened mitigation capacity can materially reduce the quality of civilian-harm review and therefore belongs in any accountability analysis.
