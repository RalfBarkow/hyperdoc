When Title-Based FedWiki Links May Be Enough

The claim that title-based HyperDoc links are insufficient is strong, but it is not always wrong to start with titles. This page qualifies the claim.

Documentation often starts from reader intent

A documentation page usually wants to point a reader toward a concept they can recognize. In that context, a page title is often the most legible identifier. Readers think in terms of FIND, WALK, or PRINT, not in terms of slugs and context stacks.

A title-based model can be good enough for a first approximation

If the goal is only to create a convenient bridge from HyperDoc into a known, already-registered FedWiki hyperbook, title-based lookup may be adequate as an interface layer. Internally, the system could still resolve that title to a slug using the site's own mappings.

In that limited case, titles can be a user-facing entry point rather than the real identity model.

Where this approximation breaks down

  • the same title exists across multiple sites
  • the title-to-slug mapping is ambiguous or stale
  • context-sensitive resolution is important
  • the goal is to model collaborative-link semantics, not just offer a shortcut

Once any of those conditions matters, title-only linking stops being a close enough representation.

Pragmatic compromise

A reasonable design may therefore have two layers:

  • a reader-friendly title-oriented surface
  • a site/slug/context-aware resolution model underneath

This would let HyperDoc stay readable without pretending that titles alone are the full operational identity.

Conclusion

Title-based links may be enough for documentation ergonomics or a first-pass UI, but they are not enough if the goal is to match the FedWiki reference implementation closely. They are useful as a surface, not sufficient as the whole model.
