Human responsibility in AI-assisted targeting

hyperdoc

AI-assisted targeting systems can help process large volumes of data quickly, but faster analysis does not remove human responsibility for target choice, civilian-harm review, or strike authorization. HyperDoc uses this page for the difference between formal human approval and substantive human accountability.

What AI-assisted means here

The relevant claim is not that software autonomously decides everything. The narrower concern is that software may rank targets, fuse signals, compress analyst time, and shape what humans see before a formal human decision is made.

Formal human decision versus substantive accountability

A human signature at the end of the workflow does not by itself prove meaningful oversight. If the human decision depends on opaque ranking, rushed review, or institutional pressure to approve, responsibility remains human but oversight may be thinner than the phrase human in the loop suggests.

Automation bias and compressed review

AI assistance can generate automation bias: humans may over-trust system outputs, especially under time pressure. That matters most when current site status, adjacency, or civilian presence should still be checked independently.

Boundary

This page does not claim that AI caused any one reported strike. It records a narrower structural question: when targeting workflows accelerate through software assistance, what safeguards preserve real human review and real accountability for harm?
