Investigating missile-origin claims

hyperdoc

Missile-origin claims are forensic questions, not only political ones. HyperDoc uses this page for the evidence disciplines that help distinguish origin hypotheses, inventory claims, and platform narratives after a disputed strike.

What missile-origin forensics uses

  • video and imagery from the strike and launch window
  • fragment or debris characteristics when available
  • known missile families, inventories, and launch envelopes
  • trajectory and angle reasoning from damage patterns
  • time-sequenced satellite or site imagery

Limits of the method

Forensic attribution can be constrained by damaged evidence, partial video, manipulated releases, limited access, and uncertainty about inventories. That means the right output is often a graded confidence claim, not a theatrical certainty claim.

Relation to public attribution

Public narrative often outruns forensic work. This page exists to preserve the slower evidentiary layer so that later reconstruction is not collapsed into the speed and incentives of first-hour blame exchange.

Boundary

This page does not promise that every strike can be conclusively attributed from open evidence. It records the narrower point that technical reconstruction should remain conceptually distinct from public accusation and denial.
