Source chain and attribution

The immediate reporting source is Infosperber's article E-IDs: Umkämpfte Studie warnt vor grossem Irrglauben by Pascal Sigg. The article reports on arguments made by Cade Diehm and the New Design Congress about digital identity risk, and it frames those arguments as relevant to the Swiss E-ID debate.

The upstream research context named by the article is the New Design Congress report and research-note cluster around The Digital Identity Event Horizon, especially Diehm's note The Mask-Off Moment for Digital Identity and Diehm/Royer's note The Digital Identity Accountability Gap. HyperDoc therefore treats this slice as a provenance chain: Infosperber report, attributed NDC arguments, then durable HyperDoc concepts.

How to read claims in this cluster

HyperDoc does not restate the article's contested claims as settled fact. The safe reading is: the article reports, Diehm argues, and the study claims. Where a page in this cluster draws a broader design lesson, it should be read as a documented critical context for discussion of public digital identity, not as a final empirical closure of the issue.

Durable concepts extracted from the article

  • Digital Identity as Abstraction: the article reports a view of digital identity as a reduction of persons into machine-readable parameters.
  • Over-Identification and Identity Creep: the article reports concern about over-identification and downstream effects of identity systems.
  • Digital Identity and Social Engineering: the article reports that many harms arise by exploiting human trust rather than by breaking cryptography.
  • Trust Erosion from Identity Fraud: the article reports harm to trust systems and social bonds.
  • The False Security of Biometrics: the article reports that biometrics are often over-trusted and can shift risk onto users.
  • Design Constraints for Public Digital Identity: the article reports recommendations for non-biometric alternatives and reachable human override paths.
  • Swiss E-ID Through the Lens of Digital Identity Risk: the article uses these claims as a critical lens for Switzerland's E-ID debate.
  • Cade Diehm and the New Design Congress: provenance page for the actor and organization making the claims.

Attributed example anchors

The article highlights recurring anchors that HyperDoc preserves as attributed examples rather than turning into free-floating claims:

  • Estonia as a frequently cited eID success story that the article and Diehm treat as riskier and more fraud-permissive than its reputation suggests.
  • Swedish Bank-ID fraud as an example where social engineering exploited trust rather than a broken cipher.
  • Israeli military use of SIM-card metadata and digital footprints as an example of digital identity abstraction under violent conditions.
  • Palantir plus ICE / DHS data fusion as an example of data aggregation and identity-linked control.
  • Biometric malware, biometric imitation, and coercive biometric attacks as examples used to question the idea that biometrics settle identity cleanly.
