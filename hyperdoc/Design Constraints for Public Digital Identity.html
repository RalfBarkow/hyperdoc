Design Constraints for Public Digital Identity

hyperdoc

The Infosperber article does not stop at critique. It reports a small set of design constraints that the New Design Congress considers especially relevant for public digital identity systems. HyperDoc records those as attributed design constraints, not as universally settled design law.

Attributed constraints reported in the article

  • Public digital identity systems should offer non-biometric alternatives with equivalent functionality, access, and protection.
  • These alternatives should not be punitive opt-outs or second-class paths.
  • Where a digital identity system controls access, a reachable human should be able to correct errors or override failures.

Why these constraints matter

The article reports these constraints because the study sees exclusion, coercion, and liability-shifting as structural risks rather than edge cases. If a system assumes biometrics, or assumes that no human correction path is needed, then failure becomes something the user must endure rather than something the institution must repair.

Swiss relevance

The article frames these points as directly relevant to Swiss E-ID debate. HyperDoc therefore treats them as design questions the Swiss project should be asked to answer clearly, not as proof that one specific design is already irredeemable.
