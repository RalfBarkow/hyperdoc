Over-Identification and Identity Creep

hyperdoc

The article reports that the study sees over-identification and downstream effects of identity systems as under-analysed. HyperDoc uses over-identification for systems that demand or infer more identity data than a task strictly needs, and identity creep for the expansion of those identity demands into additional contexts, institutions, or decision paths.

Why this is more than data minimisation

The article does not reduce the problem to one extra field or one bad form. The concern is broader: once a public or commercial infrastructure treats rich identity data as normal, more systems can start asking for it, linking it, or deriving new classifications from it.

Editorial boundary

HyperDoc does not claim that every extension of identity data use is illegitimate. The durable question is narrower: when do new identity demands create a wider control surface than the original task requires, and when do they make later misuse easier?
