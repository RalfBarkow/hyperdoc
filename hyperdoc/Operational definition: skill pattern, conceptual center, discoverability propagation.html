Operational definition: skill pattern, conceptual center, discoverability propagation

hyperdoc

A skill pattern is a reusable local documentation pattern with a fixed purpose, a bounded slice shape, and a stable acceptance rule.

In HyperDoc, the pattern documented here distinguishes four things:

  • a conceptual center that lands the local editorial center
  • discoverability propagation that adds one-click entry points from adjacent surfaces
  • a docs-only propagation slice that keeps the second move conservative
  • architectural drift when the propagation slice starts reopening theory, runtime design, or topic growth without need

The base pattern is intentionally two-step:

  1. land the conceptual center first
  2. add discoverability propagation second

A later runtime follow-up is optional and separate. It is not part of the operational definition of the base pattern.

Definitions

A conceptual center is the first slice that fixes the local editorial center for a concept in HyperDoc's own language. It may add pages and, when genuinely needed, new topics.

Discoverability propagation is the second slice that makes the new center easier to find from adjacent surfaces. It should usually add compact cross-links or orientation cues rather than new theory sections.

A docs-only propagation slice is the conservative form of that second move: page-level discoverability only, with no topic growth, no runtime claims, and no hidden architecture rewrite.

Architectural drift is the failure mode in which the second slice stops being propagation and starts widening scope into fresh conceptual center work, runtime design, or topic growth.

Worked example
