Teaching sequences and teaching arcs

hyperdoc

HyperDoc uses Teaching sequence and Teaching arc as derived pedagogical terms. DITA 1.3 does not use these exact names. The closest official structures in the learning-and-training specialization are learningObject and learningGroup.

DITA-derived mapping

The DITA learning-and-training specialization separates specialized learning topic types from map structures that order and group them. The most relevant topic types here are learningPlan, learningOverview, learningContent, learningSummary, and learningAssessment. The most relevant map structures are learningObject and learningGroup.

HyperDoc maps Teaching sequence to the reusable ordered instructional unit represented most closely by learningObject. HyperDoc maps Teaching arc to the higher-level grouping structure represented most closely by learningGroup.

Teaching sequence

A Teaching sequence is a reusable ordered instructional unit organized around one learning objective. DITA's closest analogue is learningObject, whose canonical pattern is plan, then overview or pre-assessment, then one or more content items, then summary, then one or more post-assessments. DITA also allows the content portion to reuse ordinary non-learning topics through learningContentRef.

The relevant sequencing model is ordered next/previous navigation through map structure, especially collection-type="sequence". This is not the same thing as learner-interaction sequencing inside assessment elements such as lcSequencing2 or lcSequence2.

Operations for teaching sequences

  • has-learning-objective: sequence states the learning objective that the ordered unit is meant to achieve.
  • has-plan: sequence includes planning or design intent for the unit.
  • introduces-with-overview: sequence frames objectives, prerequisites, duration, and audience.
  • pre-assesses-with: sequence may start with an optional entry checkpoint.
  • teaches-through-content: one or more content-bearing topics deliver the instruction.
  • contains-content-component: subordinate content components refine a content unit within the sequence.
  • summarizes-with: sequence includes recap and reinforcement.
  • post-assesses-with: sequence may end with an optional exit checkpoint or test.
  • precedes: sequence explicitly orders itself before another sequence in a pedagogical path.
  • follows: sequence explicitly orders itself after another sequence in a pedagogical path.
  • validates-sequence-order: sequence checks whether its internal order still follows the DITA-like instructional pattern.

Teaching arc

A Teaching arc is a higher-level pedagogical grouping of teaching sequences, or of nested arcs, used to shape lesson-, module-, or course-level progression. DITA's closest analogue is learningGroup, which groups learning objects into higher-level organizations and can itself nest.

Operations for teaching arcs

  • groups-sequence: arc contains one or more teaching sequences.
  • groups-arc: arc can nest subordinate arcs.
  • orders-children: arc preserves intended pedagogical progression among its children.
  • declares-level: arc identifies itself as lesson, module, course, or equivalent.
  • declares-entry-point: arc states where a learner should begin.
  • declares-exit-point: arc states where the grouped progression resolves or hands off.
  • aggregates-objectives: arc computes or states higher-level purpose from the contained sequences.
  • aggregates-prerequisites: arc summarizes learner prerequisites across its children.
  • aggregates-duration: arc summarizes expected duration across its children.
  • aggregates-audience: arc summarizes intended audience across its children.
  • validates-arc-coherence: arc checks that the grouped progression remains pedagogically intelligible.

Why this DITA distinction is useful in HyperDoc

The official DITA vocabulary is useful here because it separates reusable instructional objects, reusable information or content objects, higher-level grouping structures, assessment interactions, and learner-facing metadata such as audience, difficulty, and typical learning time. HyperDoc can adopt that separation as inspectable vocabulary without pretending that DITA itself uses the exact terms Teaching sequence or Teaching arc.

Bridge to Winston and Horn

Winston and Horn Lisp (1989) is a good local bridge because it preserves a classic symbolic-programming teaching sequence and then lifts that sequence into a broader teaching arc that runs from list processing and procedure abstraction through CLOS and larger AI-oriented examples. The editorial neighborhood for that progression is preserved on Winston and Horn Lisp topic arrangement.
