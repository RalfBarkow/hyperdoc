Everything in Git? Identity Across Systems

Question: The wish for complete versioning, reproducibility, and referability often appears as "everything in Git?".

Core problem: how to identify things reliably when they move across systems, instances, and communication surfaces.

Working answer

  • Identity is not absolute; it is scoped by context and protocol.
  • IDs are reliable inside an instance, but become ambiguous when exported without context anchors.
  • Cross-system referability needs both identifier and context (origin, protocol, and resolution rule).
  • Git helps with versioned artifacts, but does not by itself solve cross-system semantic identity.

Practical implications for HyperDoc and FedWiki

  • Use HyperDoc pages for durable conceptual framing and stable references.
  • Use localhost FedWiki pages for ongoing negotiation, journaling, and revisions.
  • Keep both surfaces connected by explicit topic links and counterpart pages.
  • When moving material across systems, preserve context anchors (where it came from, how to resolve it).
