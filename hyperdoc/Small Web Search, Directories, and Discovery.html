Small Web Search, Directories, and Discovery

hyperdoc

The discussion around Kagi Small Web, Marginalia, indieblog.page, blogrolls, 88x31 buttons, and webrings shows that the hard problem is not publication alone. The hard problem is discoverability without simply reconstituting the same ranking and platform structures that made independent sites hard to find in the first place.

Directories are editorial lenses, not neutral mirrors

The Kagi discussion is useful because it reveals the editorial commitments of a discovery service. Requiring feeds, preferring recent posts, excluding some hosted platforms, limiting languages, or using submission friction to control spam are not implementation details. They shape what counts as visible small web at all.

Coverage and readability pull in opposite directions

A tightly curated index can remain pleasant to browse, but it risks missing a large tail of worthy sites. A broader index improves coverage, but becomes noisier, harder to maintain, and more vulnerable to slop or spam. The thread repeatedly circles this trade-off without resolving it away.

Neighborhood discovery remains important

Several comments recover older discovery patterns: blogrolls, personal OPML files, 88x31 button gardens, directory pages, webrings, and “random page” scripts. These are not obsolete decorations. They are lightweight neighborhood-building devices that let discovery proceed by adjacency, affinity, and trust rather than by global popularity alone.

Search lenses can be independent without being complete

Marginalia and similar projects matter here because they try to rank and surface material by different assumptions than large commercial search engines. Their value is not that they replace every general search task, but that they make another discovery regime available.

Implication for HyperDoc

HyperDoc should record discovery systems as bounded lenses. A directory, a search engine, a blogroll, and a feed aggregator all help readers find the small web, but they do so through different constraints and should not be collapsed into a single abstract “search” topic.
