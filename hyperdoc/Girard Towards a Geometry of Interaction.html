Girard Towards a Geometry of Interaction

hyperdoc

This page assimilates Jean-Yves Girard's 1989 paper Towards a Geometry of Interaction as the second Girard source station in the Interaction nets neighborhood. Where the 1987 Linear Logic page anchors linear logic, proof-nets, trips, and cut elimination, this page anchors the more operational turn: proofs as actions, logic as dynamics, interfaces, plugging, and the search for a mathematical account of computation that is not merely a reconstruction of lambda calculus.

Bibliographic source

GIRARD, Jean-Yves, 1989. Towards a Geometry of Interaction. Contemporary Mathematics. Vol. 92, p. 69-88. American Mathematical Society.

Assimilation verdict

Treat this source as the conceptual bridge from linear logic as resource-sensitive proof to geometry of interaction as proof dynamics. The durable HyperDoc edge is: 

Girard 1987 linear logic -> proof-nets and cut elimination -> Girard 1989 proofs as actions -> interfaces and plugging -> later interaction nets

The paper should not be collapsed into a single Interaction nets page. It is better used as a source station for the operational vocabulary that makes interaction-net locality intelligible: action, state, transition, interface, plug, wire, cut, circularity, and non-commutative proof structure.

Page-image reading map

  • Page 69. Programmatic abstract: proof theory should be read through computer-science interaction, but without reducing the program to conventional lambda-calculus implementation.
  • Pages 70-72. Logical commitments: Platonism and intuitionism are treated as insufficient; Girard shifts the slogan from proofs are functions to proofs are actions.
  • Pages 72-75. Linear logic is presented as a logic of action. Stable truth is replaced by actions that consume, transform, or make states accessible.
  • Pages 73-75. The two conjunctions separate independent simultaneous actions from choice-bound actions; this is the operational reading of multiplicatives and additives.
  • Pages 74-76. State and transition examples include Petri nets, games, chemical reactions, and expert systems. The important semantic object is not just a proposition but reachability from one state to another.
  • Pages 76-81. Linear negation and structural rules are reread operationally: weakening discards, contraction duplicates, and exchange commutes.
  • Pages 82-85. One-sided sequent calculus and the geometry section introduce interface, cut as plugging, and extension wires as the operational surface of proof composition.
  • Pages 85-87. The non-commutative direction drops exchange, treats sequents circularly, and prepares a more local view of proof-net dynamics.
  • Pages 87-88. The PROLOG discussion begins to separate external logical truth from internal operational behavior of proof search.

HyperDoc crosswalk

  • Surfaces. A proof interface is a surface: it is where equipment can be connected, inspected, and cut against another proof object.
  • Boundaries. Cut as plugging is a transition boundary. It joins two proof interfaces and turns a logical composition into an execution step.
  • State machines. The paper's state/transition reading maps directly to HyperDoc's run trace vocabulary: initial state, accessible state, transition, guard, and evidence.
  • Chunk coherence. Weakening, contraction, and exchange become explicit permissions for discarding, duplicating, or commuting resources. This fits HyperDoc's insistence on making dependencies inspectable rather than implicit.
  • Interaction nets. The safe bridge remains indirect: proof dynamics and plugging lead toward local interaction, but the 1989 source is still a Geometry of Interaction source, not an Interaction nets definition.
