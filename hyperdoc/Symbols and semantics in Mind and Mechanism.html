Symbols and semantics in Mind and Mechanism

hyperdoc

Chapter 5 of Mind and Mechanism is the narrowest reusable seam from the book into HyperDoc. McDermott argues there that symbols in computational systems do not become interesting merely because an observer decodes them; the important case is when symbol structures stand in causal relations to the environment and participate in behavior.

Source-backed claims

  • Informational meaning is presented as the case where an event counts as evidence about a situation by changing that situation's probability.
  • The chapter proposes that intentionality may be explainable through thicker causal and informational relations than observer-relative labels alone.
  • It argues that semantics applies to symbol structures and their compositional organization, not merely to isolated tokens.
  • It makes behavior central: data structures become philosophically interesting here when they alter what the system does next.

Bridge to grounded retrieval

Aleksander & Morton 1993 adds a narrow bridge that HyperDoc can reuse without changing its voice. Their iconic hypothesis treats language as a vehicle for retrieving richer world-state or iconic representation, and treats understanding as successful retrieval rather than as arbitrary symbol attachment alone. HyperDoc's route language gives that bridge a local form: a station can stand for a grounded semantic station, and a route can stand for the retrieval relation by which a cue re-enters evidence, trajectory, or interpretation.

That does not replace the symbolic question in Mind and Mechanism. It sharpens it. If symbol structures matter because they change what the system does next, one strong way for them to matter is by retrieving the grounded state that lets later behavior count as understanding instead of mere decoding. HyperDoc's contribution is not a neural implementation claim, but a documentation-native retrieval language for inspecting those semantic paths explicitly.
