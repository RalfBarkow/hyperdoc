Symbols and semantics in Mind and Mechanism

hyperdoc

Chapter 5 of Mind and Mechanism is the narrowest reusable seam from the book into HyperDoc. McDermott argues there that symbols in computational systems do not become interesting merely because an observer decodes them; the important case is when symbol structures stand in causal relations to the environment and participate in behavior.

Inspectable objects

  • Symbols and semantics
  • Informational meaning
  • Intentionality

Compatibility points

  • The book treats intentionality as the capacity of a state to be about something and proposes that it may be approached through causal and informational relations rather than through observer-imposed labels alone.
  • It introduces informational meaning as the case where an event provides evidence about a situation by changing that situation's probability.
  • It argues that semantics applies to symbol structures and their compositional behavior, not only to isolated tokens.
  • It makes behavior central: data structures matter because they help drive what the system does next.
