Core concepts from Lafont 1990

  • Interaction nets are undirected graphs with labelled vertices (agents/symbols) and fixed ports per symbol.
  • Each symbol has one distinguished principal port.
  • An active/alive pair is two agents connected by principal ports.
  • Rules are local binary interactions on active pairs.
  • Constraint stack: linearity, binary interaction, no ambiguity, optimisation, typing, completeness, and simplicity.
  • Typing gives local correctness and matching on principal ports.
  • Simplicity / semi-simplicity blocks vicious-circle deadlock.
  • Sequential runtime hint: encode nets as cells and pointers, store active pairs in a stack, no language-level garbage collector needed under linearity.

Figure 9 rule DSL mapping

Lafont writes interactions with )(. The seed keeps a first declarative, S-expression layer: 

((cons x (append u t))
 (append v (cons x t)))

(nil (append v v))

These two specs are compiled by define-lafont-rule into runtime rewrites for the Figure 9 list append fragment.
