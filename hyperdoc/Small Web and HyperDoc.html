Small Web and HyperDoc

hyperdoc

The small web matters to HyperDoc not as nostalgia but as a reference system for human-scale publishing, explicit linking, and non-platform discovery. The supplied article and discussion treat the small web as a social and technical formation built from personal sites, feeds, blogrolls, directories, search lenses, and selective protocol constraints rather than from one single software stack.

Why it belongs in HyperDoc

HyperDoc already tracks reference systems, arrangement pages, and boundary statements rather than pretending all adjacent systems are equivalent. The small web should be imported in the same way: as a cluster of reusable topics and a small set of durable pages, not as a single flattened essay or a dump of thread fragments.

The material is especially relevant because it keeps returning to questions HyperDoc also faces: how independent publishing remains discoverable, how curation differs from platform capture, how explicit links and local neighborhoods compete with ranking systems, and how technical simplification can change social incentives.

The central claim

The small web is not best defined by absolute size. In the supplied material, it is better understood as a publishing ethos and discovery ecology oriented toward personal, non-commercial, lightly mediated sites. What makes it “small” is not that it contains only a few thousand documents, but that it preserves weaker commercial pressure, more legible authorship, and more human-scale discovery paths than the dominant ad-driven web.

Main subproblems surfaced by the material

  • aggregation and human reading limits
  • directories, feeds, and inclusion criteria
  • Gemini versus ordinary browser-based web publishing
  • search and ranking without SEO or popularity lock-in
  • intrinsic motivation versus monetization pressure
  • tracking, cookies, and protocol boundary choices
