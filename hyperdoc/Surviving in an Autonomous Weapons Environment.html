Surviving in an Autonomous Weapons Environment

This page frames survival as a civilian resilience problem, not a combat optimization problem. The focus is on reducing harm, preserving social function, and keeping human accountability.

Core principles

  • Protect life first: prevention, shelter, evacuation, and medical continuity.
  • Decentralize critical services: avoid single points of failure for power, comms, and care.
  • Keep humans in the loop for governance decisions and emergency exceptions.
  • Design for degraded conditions: intermittent power, broken networks, uncertain information.
  • Document everything: incident logs, chain-of-custody, and post-incident review artifacts.

What changes in autonomous-threat conditions

  • Decision speed increases, so warning/response windows can shrink.
  • Misinformation pressure rises; verification and trusted channels matter more.
  • Infrastructure and logistics become primary targets for disruption.
  • Psychological strain becomes persistent; mental-health operations are part of survival.

Minimum viable resilience stack

  1. Community alert protocol with fallback channels.
  2. Shelter and movement plan for non-combatants.
  3. Medical triage and continuity mapping.
  4. Data integrity and offline record procedures.
  5. Recovery workflow for services and institutions.
