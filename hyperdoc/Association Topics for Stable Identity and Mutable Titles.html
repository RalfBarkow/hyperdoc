Association Topics for Stable Identity and Mutable Titles

hyperdoc

In HyperDoc, not every relation deserves durable topic identity. When a relation becomes a subject of review, it should first be promoted into a reviewed topic proposal object. That proposal can carry a candidate :id, :title, :summary, editorial references, and an exact-title merge classification without automatically editing hyperdoc/topics.lisp or creating new page files.

Current HyperDoc rule

The reviewed promotion step exists because authored topic factories in hyperdoc/topics.lisp are durable reconstruction points. A live relation object can propose a topic, but it must not silently materialize one.

  • Use a reviewed proposal when the relation itself has become a subject.
  • Use exact canonical topic title as the practical merge key.
  • Do not add a second factory with the same exact :title.

This specification defines how immutable slugs or stable keys and mutable titles can be represented in a DMX-style topic-map model in which associations are also first-class topics. The goal is to preserve subject identity while allowing names, titles, and role bindings to change over time without turning every rename into a deletion-and-recreation event.

1. Problem

A title change should not imply that the underlying subject has ceased to exist. In current title-keyed page systems, a rename changes the lookup target and therefore breaks inbound links. The desired model is stronger: the subject keeps one stable identity, while one or more mutable naming or presentation relations can be revised, journaled, and undone.

In topic-map terms, this means the system must not reduce identity to a current label string. The stable reference point must remain constant across different names, pages, views, and times.

2. Core modeling rule

Everything important in this model is a topic, including the relation between a stable identity and a mutable title.

  • The durable subject is a topic.
  • A title or name is also a topic when it needs independent addressability, history, or reuse.
  • The relation between them is an association topic, not a hidden field.

This makes title-bearing relations inspectable, nameable, versionable, and further relatable. It also avoids collapsing identity into whichever string happens to be displayed today.

3. Association-topic pattern

The relevant association topic type is a relation between a subject with stable identity and a title-like representation that may change over time.

3.1 Mandatory participants

  • Association topic: the first-class topic representing the identity/title relation.
  • Stable subject topic: the enduring subject, identified by immutable slug or stable key.
  • Title topic: the current or historical title representation.

3.2 Player roles

In DMX terms, the stable subject topic and title topic occupy player roles in the association topic. The exact side assignment is a policy choice; the important invariant is that the role semantics are explicit and stable.

  • player1 may be defined as identified-subject.
  • player2 may be defined as presented-title.

Or the system may invert them. What must not vary is the meaning of each role within a given association type. A reader or tool should never need to infer role semantics from accidental position alone.

Recommended convention: use player1 = stable subject and player2 = mutable title. That keeps the durable thing on the first side and the revisable representation on the second side.

3.3 Identity invariants

  • The stable subject topic keeps one immutable slug or stable key for its entire lifetime.
  • The association topic also has its own stable identity.
  • The title topic may be replaced, superseded, or historical without changing the stable subject topic.
  • A title change is modeled as a change in the association state, not as replacement of the subject.

4. Why the association itself must be a topic

Treating the relation as a first-class topic makes it possible to attach its own metadata and lifecycle. Examples include:

  • valid-from / valid-to
  • preferred / deprecated / historical status
  • editorial rationale
  • source of authority
  • migration notes
  • undo / redo lineage

Without a first-class association topic, these become awkward hidden fields on either the subject or the title, and the system loses the ability to talk about the relation itself as a subject of inspection and repair.

5. Minimum object model

StableSubjectTopic
  - stable_key            ; immutable slug / durable identity
  - current_status
  - occurrences
  - associations

TitleTopic
  - text
  - language
  - status                ; candidate / current / deprecated / historical
  - occurrences

IdentityTitleAssociationTopic
  - association_id        ; stable identity of the relation itself
  - association_type      ; e.g. subject-has-title
  - player1               ; stable subject topic
  - player2               ; title topic
  - role1_type            ; identified-subject
  - role2_type            ; presented-title
  - status                ; active / superseded / historical
  - valid_from
  - valid_to
  - journal_ref
  - predecessor
  - successor

6. Interpretation rule

A system view that needs a current display title for a subject must resolve it by following the active identity/title association, not by assuming that the stable key and current title are the same string.

  • Lookup by identity uses the stable subject key.
  • Presentation uses the current active title association.
  • Historical inspection may show older title associations.

7. Rename semantics

A rename is defined as a bounded update to the title side of the identity/title association. It does not replace the stable subject topic.

There are two valid implementation styles:

  1. Retitle in place: keep the same association topic and update its player2 binding to a new title topic.
  2. Supersede by new association: close the old association and create a new active association topic with the same stable subject topic and a new title topic.

The second style is preferred when full historical traceability matters, because the old and new title relations remain separately inspectable.

8. Consequences for page systems

A page-like system that renders a subject should not use the visible title as its sole durable lookup key. Instead:

  • URLs, slugs, and durable lookups should follow the stable subject key.
  • Visible headings should come from the current active title association.
  • Old titles should remain inspectable as historical title topics or historical associations.

This prevents a rename from becoming a path migration by default. Path migration becomes optional, not mandatory.

9. Required guarantees

  • One stable key identifies the enduring subject.
  • Role semantics for player1 and player2 are explicit per association type.
  • The association topic is itself addressable and inspectable.
  • Title changes are recorded as changes to association state, not destruction of the subject.
  • Historical title relations remain available for audit and repair.

10. Recommended naming policy

Use the phrase immutable slug, mutable title only as a surface summary. The deeper model is:

  • stable subject identity
  • journaled presentation relations
  • association topics with explicit player roles

That formulation scales beyond page titles to aliases, external names, multilingual labels, and later representation forms.

11. HyperDoc merge guidance

Operationally, the exact canonical title remains the merge key in HyperDoc's current Topics model. If a relation proposal says :merge-into-existing-topic, search hyperdoc/topics.lisp for that exact :title string and edit the existing factory in place. Add a new factory only when the proposal says the exact title is new.
