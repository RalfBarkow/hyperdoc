Computationalism in Mind and Mechanism

hyperdoc

Mind and Mechanism is computationalist in a specific sense that is useful to HyperDoc: it treats computation as the explanatory route into mind, while refusing to collapse that route into one tiny algorithm or one finished AI system. The book's preface and its chapters on artificial intelligence and consciousness repeatedly push toward computational explanation, modular mechanisms, and inspectable information-processing structures.

Source-backed claims

  • The book explicitly defends a version of computationalism: mind is to be explained in terms of computation rather than by a noncomputational remainder.
  • It treats computation as physically realized information processing, not as mere observer-relative notation.
  • Cognitive science is presented as the study of computational models of the tasks brains perform.
  • The self-model discussion matters here because some apparently introspective phenomena are re-described through the models a system uses of itself.
