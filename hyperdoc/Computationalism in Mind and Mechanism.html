Computationalism in Mind and Mechanism

hyperdoc

Mind and Mechanism is computationalist in a specific sense that is useful to HyperDoc: it treats computation as the explanatory route into mind, while refusing to collapse that route into one tiny algorithm or one finished AI system. The book's preface and its chapters on artificial intelligence and consciousness repeatedly push toward computational explanation, modular mechanisms, and inspectable information-processing structures.

Inspectable objects

  • Computationalism

Compatibility points

  • Computation is treated as physically realized information processing, not as mere observer-relative notation.
  • The explanatory target is not only deduction or theorem proving, but also the layered and uneven mechanisms that make intelligent behavior possible.
  • The book's self-model discussion matters here because it treats some apparently introspective phenomena through the models a system uses of itself.

Why this matters to the existing Drew cluster

HyperDoc already preserves Drew McDermott's software and planning work as a concept-and-software cluster. This page adds the minimal conceptual claim that makes that cluster compatible with Mind and Mechanism: the preserved Lisp, planning, and representational artifacts can be read as computational explanation surfaces, not just as historical downloads.
