Why this belongs in the cluster

The confessional example matters because it frames privacy not merely as secrecy but as a social architecture for truthful speech. The source text's claim is that people sometimes need a trusted setting in which they can speak or prove something significant without the whole content becoming public.

What the example contributes

The confessional is relevant here because it highlights a durable design problem: how can an institution invite honest disclosure while sharply constraining who can hear, keep, or misuse that disclosure?

That makes it a useful historical neighbor for zero knowledge proofs even though the mechanism is social and religious rather than mathematical. The continuity is not formal equivalence. The continuity is the recurring need for trustworthy expression under conditions of non-exposure.

Relation to existing HyperDoc work

This page is most closely related to Design Constraints for Public Digital Identity. Public digital identity systems are often discussed as if proof and exposure were inseparable. The confessional example helps keep another possibility in view: institutions can be designed around protected disclosure instead of maximal disclosure.

Editorial boundary

This page does not endorse confessional institutions as such, and it does not claim that privacy is always best understood through confession. It preserves the narrower historical analogy: trusted speech sometimes requires architecture that sharply limits disclosure.
