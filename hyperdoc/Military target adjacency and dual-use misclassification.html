Military target adjacency and dual-use misclassification

hyperdoc

A civilian site near a military site can inherit risk through mapping, classification, or stale interpretive assumptions. HyperDoc uses this page for the elevated danger created when adjacency is mistaken for identity, or when a civilian structure is read through the history of a nearby military facility.

Why adjacency complicates target classification

Nearby military activity can make a civilian building appear suspicious in imagery, inherited databases, or simplified maps. That becomes more dangerous when current civilian use is not actively rechecked.

Dual-use and inherited labels

Dual-use language can be overextended. A location may once have had military relevance, or may sit beside a military facility, without that making every neighboring civilian structure a valid target. Classification needs present evidence, not only historical association.

Why adjacency is not target authorization

The durable rule is simple: proximity to a military site does not by itself make a civilian site targetable. Adjacency raises the need for more careful verification, not less.

Boundary

This page does not deny that some sites genuinely are dual-use. It records a narrower warning: adjacency and old labels can create false confidence, which is exactly why target validation must be current and site-specific.
