Precision weapons and wrong-target failure

hyperdoc

HyperDoc uses this page for a simple but important distinction: a weapon can be highly accurate in flight while still being aimed at the wrong place. The failure is then in target selection, target description, or target-data freshness rather than in the munition's guidance system.

Accurate weapon versus correct target

Public discussion often collapses these into one idea. HyperDoc keeps them separate. A guidance system answers whether the weapon reached the supplied point. Targeting review answers whether that supplied point was the correct one to strike.

Common wrong-target pathways

  • coordinates remain in the system after the site changes use
  • adjacent sites are conflated in mapping or interpretation
  • a site keeps an inherited label from an earlier phase of conflict
  • reviewers assume prior approval still settles present legality

Why precision does not excuse the review failure

The rhetorical force of precision-guided language can obscure the upstream chain of responsibility. Precision matters for delivery. It does not remove the need for recent intelligence, current site classification, and credible civilian-harm review.

Boundary

This page is not an anti-precision argument. It instead prevents a category mistake: a precise strike system is not the same thing as a reliably correct target-selection system.
