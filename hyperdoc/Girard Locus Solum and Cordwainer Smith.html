Girard Locus Solum and Cordwainer Smith

hyperdoc

This page assimilates Jean-Yves Girard's 2001 monograph Locus Solum: From the rules of logic to the logic of rules as the third Girard source station in the HyperDoc interaction neighborhood. The durable edge is no longer only proof-nets or geometry of interaction; it is the move into ludics: designs, locations, behaviours, orthogonality, incarnation, disputes, and the operational principle that the rules themselves induce a logic.

Bibliographic sources

Operational thesis for HyperDoc

In HyperDoc terms, ludics is the discipline of making a claim's interaction surface primary. A topic, operation, state-machine trace, or source station is not only judged by internal syntax or external semantics. It is judged by what it can interact with, where it is located, what counts as an opposing or testing object, and what part of it survives as its incarnation inside a behaviour. 

source object -> located design
review/test object -> counter-design
normalisation -> interaction run
orthogonality -> convergent agreement between design and counter-design
behaviour -> type/contract/game formed by biorthogonal closure
incarnation -> the materially relevant part of an artifact in that behaviour
dispute -> trace of interaction through the net

Relation to Cordwainer Smith

Smith gives Girard a literary machine in which a tiny located event becomes a world- changing computation: a ruby trembles, correction machinery fails, a diamond records the error, and both error and correction enter the general computer. The resulting person, Elaine, is a mistake inside a well-ordered programming regime; through the D'joan story and the underpeople, that mistake exposes what the regime cannot see.

The relation to Locus Solum is therefore operational, not decorative:

  • Location. Smith begins at An-fang, the beginning place; Girard makes locus the primary logical fact.
  • Programming bureaucracy. Smith's People Programmer stages a computational order that can process error and correction without understanding their consequence.
  • Mistake. Girard's daimon is a formally improper but necessary move; Smith's mistake is not mere noise but the event that makes hidden structure visible.
  • Non-formal intelligence. Girard's dictionary entry on Smith values mistake as part of intelligence and rejects the identification of intelligence with formality.
  • Excluded participants. Smith's underpeople expose the boundary of the system's official community; ludics similarly asks which counter-designs, tests, and interactions are admitted by a behaviour.

For HyperDoc, this becomes a reusable guardrail: do not treat an artifact's validity as only a local syntactic property. Ask what it normalises with, what it refuses, what its counter-designs reveal, and what material incarnation remains after irrelevant context is removed.

Reading map

  • Pages 301-304. Epigraph, alternative titles, and the shift from rules of logic to logic of rules. Girard names ludics and glosses Locus Solum as the thesis that only location matters.
  • Pages 305-315. Designs: biases, loci, pitchforks, designs as dessins/desseins, daimon, fax, chronicles, coherence, and partial designs.
  • Pages 316-326. Normalisation: cut-nets, closed and open normalisation, slices, mauls, pull-backs, and disputes.
  • Pages 326-337. Behaviours: orthogonality, separation, stability, associativity, closure, monotonicity, incarnation, and the game/syntax/semantics readings.
  • Pages 337-363. Connectives, additives, multiplicatives, delocation, shift, quantifiers, and prenex phenomena.
  • Pages 363-371. Uniformity and bihaviours, adding PER-like structure to behaviours.
  • Pages 372-393. Truth, completeness, soundness, and full completeness of MALL2.
  • Appendices and dictionary. The bestiary/dictionary supplies Girard's informal conceptual cross-references, including the explicit Cordwainer Smith entry.

HyperDoc crosswalk

  • Topic factories. A topic constructor is a small design-like artifact: it has a stable locus/id, a visible title, a summary, and references that determine its interaction surface.
  • Review packets. A reviewable assimilation packet is a behaviour: it says what a valid source-station assimilation must interact with and survive.
  • State-machine traces. A dispute is the ludic cousin of a trace through an interaction; it records the active path rather than only the final outcome.
  • Source relevance. Incarnation is the correct word for stripping an imported source down to what is materially relevant for a specific HyperDoc behaviour.
  • Failure surfaces. Daimon/Faith map to explicit stop/give-up/divergence surfaces. They should not be hidden as generic errors.
