Expectation preserved

  • Expectation: the twelve note topics carrying the exhaustive workspace readout made it reasonable to talk about each part as a chunk and to import ChatGPT's transport definition directly.
  • Observed reality: HyperDoc already reserves bare Chunk for the running-image coherence model, while the workspace transport objects are note topics that carry parts of an exchange artifact.
  • Why the expectation was plausible: the live manifest and note keys really do partition one larger artifact into part-01 through part-12.
  • Classification: disappointed expectation at the workspace naming boundary, not at the running-image coherence model.
  • Resolution: keep Chunk for coherence semantics and do not use bare chunk for transport in this repository.

Difference from ChatGPT's chunk wording

  • ChatGPT used chunk to mean a bounded, lossless partition of a larger payload.
  • HyperDoc already uses Chunk to mean an inspectable unit whose freshness can be maintained relative to a basis and update basis.
  • Those two meanings are both operationally useful, but they are not the same kind of object.
  • In this repository, do not use bare chunk for transport; use chunk note for the topicmap transport object and exchange artifact for the transported whole.
  • A chunk note may carry one partition of a larger payload, but that does not make the note itself a HyperDoc Chunk unless it is separately modeled in the coherence system.

Operational roles

  • Exchange artifact: a larger transport object that must be exchanged through the topicmap as multiple notes.
  • Manifest note: the authoritative index note for one exchange artifact; it says what the whole artifact is, how many parts it has, what each part covers, and whether the partition is lossless.
  • Chunk note: a dmx.notes.note topic used as a transport container for exactly one declared part of an exchange artifact. It is infrastructure, not primary subject matter.
  • Content topic: a normal workspace topic whose purpose is substantive knowledge. If a chunk note contains a readout of topic 907120, authority for that subject still remains topic 907120.

The current exhaustive workspace readout for topicmap 919822 is one exchange artifact with one manifest note and twelve chunk notes. The transport notes preserve a large readout without turning those transport notes into the authoritative subject matter. Topics such as 907120 and 921464 remain the content topics.

Rules for the workspace transport layer

  1. Do not use bare chunk for transport in this repository. Use chunk note for the topicmap transport object.
  2. Every exchange artifact has exactly one manifest note. The manifest is the entrypoint.
  3. Every chunk note must declare coverage. At minimum it identifies the artifact, the part number and total count, and what it covers, such as topic ids or ranges.
  4. Chunk notes are transport-layer topics. They are durable enough for replay and audit, but they are not automatically conceptual notes.
  5. Content topics remain authoritative. Chunk notes preserve exchanged representations; they do not replace the source topics.
  6. Promotion is explicit. If chunk exchange yields durable semantic conclusions, promote them into a normal content topic rather than leaving them only inside chunk notes.

Operational definition

A chunk note is a topicmap-native transport note that stores one declared part of a larger exchange artifact, indexed by a manifest note, while content topics remain the authoritative semantic objects.

Handling model

  • Content topics: the ideas.
  • Exchange artifact: the transported whole.
  • Manifest note: the index.
  • Chunk notes: the parts.
  • Derived summaries and audits: new content topics only if promoted.
