Federated Search Without a Central Index

Ward's item SEARCH ITEMS cf80b0444c62866e finds a story item on other sites. Who answers that question? Three searches exist around Federated Wiki, and they answer it differently. Their sources are retained here at fixed revisions: the retained sources.

The neighborhood search

The client searches the sites in the reader's current neighborhood, in the browser: neighborhood.search in fedwiki/wiki-client. It asks one index per site.

  • The index normally comes from the site itself, as system/site-index.json. The server builds it from each page's title and text.
  • When that index is missing, the client builds one from the sitemap's synopses.
  • The text is taken from paragraph, markdown, html, reference and a few other item types.

Item ids are not indexed, and neither is the text of search items. A neighborhood search for cf80b0444c62866e finds pages whose text mentions the id, not pages that hold the item. Its search space is the neighborhood: no further site is asked.

The Search plugin and the search service

The Search plugin parses its text into a request, for example find: items, query: cf80b0444c62866e: parse. It sends the request to /match of a search service: search. That service is query.search.federatedwiki.org unless the item names another one in its site field.

With the word SEARCH in its text, the item asks only when its button is pressed. Without it, the item asks as soon as it is shown: handle.

The service is Ward's WardCunningham/search:

  • Schedule: Every six hours its crawler reads the sitemap of each site it already holds. It then fetches the pages that changed since the last run: the incremental scrape.
  • What it indexes: it writes the words, links, sites, item ids and plugin types of every page. For item ids it writes the alias, else the id.
  • Rollup: Rollup combines these lists per site and for the whole index.
  • Answer: /match first picks the sites whose combined list holds the query, then their pages: sites and pages. It answers with slugs and the sites that hold them: references.

The answer comes from the service's own index, as fresh as its last crawl. The data stays federated, because every site remains the authority for its pages. Which sites a search reaches is decided by one index.

How the service learns of new sites

The crawler does not only read the sites it knows; it also discovers sites.

  • From every page it records the sites the page names: the site of a reference or other item, the domains of a roster, the site of a journal entry such as a fork.
  • found.rb compares all named sites with the sites it holds. It adds the missing ones, except local addresses, so the next crawl reads them.

Discovery therefore exists, and it follows links, rosters and forks from the sites already known. It runs in one place, though, and its result is one index.

An item id search over the retained pages

This companion holds Wiki pages of three sites: the search space.

  • 110 pages of discourse.dreyeck.ch, every page of its retained sitemap;
  • Ward's Discourse Graphs page;
  • Curiosity Token.

Over these pages HyperDoc can search for an item id itself, with the crawler's rule (alias, else id), and without any request. The search keeps three things apart:

  • what was asked;
  • where it looked;
  • which story items it observed.

The results:

  • ITEMS cf80b0444c62866e observes one occurrence, on Curiosity Token. Only that page of found.ward.fed.wiki is retained, so this says nothing about the rest of that site or the federation.
  • ITEMS 31bd93ad173f651c, Ward's sentence about a distributed graph, occurs on two sites: ward.dojo.fed.wiki and discourse.dreyeck.ch, carried by a fork. The search as a Topicmap shows both occurrences as separate items, related by their shared id. From either one, the Workspace can be reprojected onto the projection of its page.
  • ITEMS 29c28902048db774, the claim fold of the Claim Template, occurs on 42 pages of discourse.dreyeck.ch. The template carries its item ids along, like a curiosity token.

What the central index would answer is not recorded. A recorded answer is the next step: it would show occurrences the index knows and this space does not, and occurrences the index reports that a page no longer holds. An index entry is a report; an item observed in a story is an occurrence. The two stay apart.

What independent discovery would need

The retained pages already name further sites. By the crawler's rule they name 160 sites outside the search space. The most frequent is wiki.ralfbarkow.ch, named by 44 pages. None of them is fetched here. To search them without a central index, a reader would need to:

  1. choose which of them to include;
  2. fetch their sitemaps and pages;
  3. repeat the step for the sites those pages name.

That is the crawler's loop, run for one reader's chosen neighborhood instead of for one index.

Three properties, not one

Federated data, federated search and decentralized discovery are different properties:

  • A search service can run in one place and still report on federated data.
  • Independent searches can exist and still miss sources they never discover.

What this reading can show is where each search looks, what it observes, and what it does not reach.
