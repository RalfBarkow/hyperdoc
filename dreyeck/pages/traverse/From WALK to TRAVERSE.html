From WALK to TRAVERSE

Ward proposed browsing the graph with WALK. On Discourse Graphs on discourse.dreyeck.ch his program changed step by step; its journal records each step. Then a second command was added beside it.

On 2026-10-20 a second item was written: a TRAVERSE program. Its first text.

WALK selects

Ward's walk_emit knows ways such as steps, days, hubs, lineup and references, but no ways named after roles. installDiscourse wraps WALK: a role way goes to role_walk_emit, and every other way to Ward's code unchanged.

walks picks pages that have an EXTRACT edge of the role. It prefers pages in the lineup, sorts them by sitemap date and takes the count. For each picked page it draws a graph from the sitemap: back links as parents, links as children. These relations carry no type. A remark on the probe page says the same: WALK acts "as a selector", not as a traversal.

The replay of this program on the retained pages gives:

  • WALK 60 questions ⇒ 6 aspects, 29 nodes;
  • WALK 30 claims ⇒ 3 aspects, 15 nodes.

Only the program's own page was in the lineup, and it has no question edges, so walks fell back to every page.

TRAVERSE follows

traverse_emit works from typed edges instead. rootOf takes the page that holds the Mech item as the root. Then the edges go through three functions:

  • resolveEdges looks each target up in the sitemap of its declared site and never substitutes another site;
  • traverse follows the listed roles breadth-first;
  • presentGraph builds a graph whose relations are typed by role.

The difference is the starting point. WALK starts from a selection of the neighborhood. TRAVERSE starts from the page it stands on, and it finds nothing when that page has no typed links.

How TRAVERSE is installed and bundled is the technical reading How TRAVERSE Enters Mech. The Implementation/Execution projection shows the wrapper, both commands and their replays.
