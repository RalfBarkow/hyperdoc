What Did Ward Propose?

Ward Cunningham's page Discourse Graphs on ward.dojo.fed.wiki starts the experiment. The page was written on 2025-11-21. This reading holds the version fetched on 2026-10-21 and checks its SHA-256 when it loads: Ward's page as retained. Links to a live Wiki open the current version, which may differ from the retained one.

A graph spread over cooperating sites

Ward's central sentence is story item 31bd93ad173f651c. He proposes the discourse graph "as a distributed graph across cooperating federated wiki sites". The schema lives in one page, Claim Template. Its PageFolds mark paragraphs and links as "question", "claim", "support" or "oppose".

The next item places the whole graph in the sitemaps. A link's type comes from the fold it sits in. A ClaimLinkSurvey would keep this classification up to date. A third item proposes browsing as an extension of Mech's WALK block: first assemble a neighborhood, then retrieve the most recent questions or claims as aspects.

His Mech program says exactly that: CLICK / NEIGHBORS / ClaimLinkSurvey / WALK 10 questions / WALK 30 claims / CLICK / SOLO.

Three stages

Ward expected collaborative software to be hard to test without collaborators, so he proposed three stages:

  1. A pod of at least three collaborators, the Claim Template, and a site for each.
  2. The Claim Link Survey Probe and a weekly review of its results.
  3. Mech WALK functions, once the pod holds ten questions and thirty claims.

Where it was taken up

Claim Template was written on 2025-12-02: the retained template. Its notes ask authors to keep the four fold names exactly and to put links inside paragraphs, "so surveys can classify edges".

The Experiment/History projection starts at Ward's sentence. It separates four kinds of association:

  • links written in a page;
  • journal history;
  • edges derived from a fold;
  • claims nobody has checked.
